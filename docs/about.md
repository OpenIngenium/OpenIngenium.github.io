# About Open Ingenium

## Current Release

Current Release Version: 15.0.0
Current Development Version: 15.1.0

## Genesis

Following the [SMAP](https://smap.jpl.nasa.gov/) and the [Mars Science Laboratory](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/msl-curiosity/) integration and test (I&T) engineers at the Jet Propulsion Laboratory found that the formats and methods for test procedures could vary widely across different teams and test venues. This existed even when all of the teams/venues were focused on testing the spacecraft at the same (system) level.

This was due to a number of reasons including:
- Team Skill Set (primarily related to software developmenent skills)
- Fidelity of test venue (simulator vs. hardware vs. flight hardware)
- Procedures and Policies of the teams (often linked to the hardware under test)
and resulted in a lack of consistency and standardization across the teams and venues.

Several early attempts were made to standardize using "low code" methods to implement test procedures including [Robot Framework](https://robotframework.org/) and an in-house, Microsoft Excel based system. These provided benefit but lacked the support and the scalability to meet the wider needs of the teams.

Ingenium was created to address these challenges by providing a standardized, scalable, and flexible framework for test procedure management.

## History

- Ingenium began development in late 2017 and was first deployed to system test venus in 2019. 
- In 2021 it was cleared for use with Flight Hardware and was deployed for use on the [Psyche](https://psyche.ssl.berkeley.edu/) and [Europa Clipper](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/europa-clipper/) Assembly Test and Launch Campaigns (ATLO).
- Ingenium was released as open source on July 31, 2025
